Welcome to SCG-AI 2027

How can software communities govern participation, responsibility, and collective decisions in the age of AI?

SCG-AI brings researchers, community maintainers, tool builders, and practitioners together to connect research evidence with practical governance challenges.

Two connected perspectives

Governing AI participation
How should communities set rules for AI-assisted contributions and autonomous agents, assign responsibility, and maintain trust?
AI for community governance
How can AI support community decisions, contributor participation, and sustainable maintenance with human oversight?

Our aim: identify shared governance challenges and develop a research agenda grounded in community needs.

02 / TOPICS OF INTEREST

Topics of Interest

We welcome evidence, methods, tools, and practical experiences that help communities govern participation, contributions, and collective decisions.

01

Community policies & contribution practices

AI-use policies, disclosure requirements, contribution rules, informal norms, and participation in community rule-making.

02

Decision rights, oversight & accountability

Permissions for people and agents, human oversight, maintenance responsibilities, auditing, appeals, and escalation.

03

Contributor participation & recognition

Onboarding and retention, mentoring, attribution of human contributions, contributor progression, and inclusive incentives.

04

Trust, transparency & legitimacy

Disclosure and provenance, explanations of decisions, perceived fairness, stakeholder trust, and responses to trust violations.

05

Maintenance workload & community sustainability

Review demand, distribution of maintenance effort, contribution-volume policies, maintainer succession, and knowledge continuity.

06

Governance infrastructure, tools & evaluation

Policy-aware workflows, compliance checks, AI-supported decisions with human oversight, community analytics, and evaluation.

A community-centered scope

Submissions should explain implications for managing contributions, supporting participation, allocating responsibilities, or making collective decisions. Empirical and exploratory studies are welcome; AI need not be part of the research method.

REVIEW PROCESS

Review criteria

Submissions will be assessed for relevance, clarity, originality, appropriate evidence, and potential to stimulate discussion.

PUBLICATION & PARTICIPATION

A forum for emerging work

We intend to publish accepted extended abstracts in the ACM Digital Library through FSE’s proceedings arrangements. FSE’s call identifies extended abstracts of up to five pages as exempt from APCs.

At least one author must register and present in person, subject to FSE’s emergency provisions. Registered participants without an accepted paper are also welcome.

Scope and publication policies +

Work focused solely on individual tool usability, model performance, generic agent coordination, or organization-wide AI adoption should establish a clear connection to software community governance.

Organizers and their directly supervised students, postdocs, and employees may not submit papers to this workshop.

As a published ACM author, you and your co-authors are subject to all ACM Publications Policies, including ACM’s new Publications Policy on Research Involving Human Participants and Subjects.
